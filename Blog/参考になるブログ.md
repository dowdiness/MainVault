I hate to say this, but this notion is actually taught in the elementary school in Japan. 

If you look up 以上 in the dictionary, you might get the impression that it has a vague meaning. This is partly true when it comes to figurative speech, but when it comes to numbers, 以上 has an explicit meaning, which serves the purpose of mathematical rigor.

What really bothers Japanese speakers is the lack of a proper antonym of 未満. You can use ~を超える, but since it's a verb rather than a noun, you’re stuck with a structural inconsistency.

I recommend reading this Japanese article if you have at least N2-level reading ability.
